## 3. Methods

**Data:**
- nflverse play-by-play for 2023-2026 (2026 through the Week 10 Thursday game), snap counts and weekly rosters, all read from local parquet files. I did not touch any PFF data.
- News from web searches: the Saturday injury report (Freiermuth questionable with illness, Harmon questionable/IR-return, CIN out: Iosivas, Dugger, Brian Parker II, Jalen Davis), the backfield-split reporting and game previews.
- The game spread and total only.

**Steps:**
1. **Team volume.** I set team volume from the spread and total, then built a correlated game-script variable: CIN margin ~ N(+3.5, 13.5). Each point of margin moves pass attempts by 0.22 and RB carries by 0.15, in the trailing-team-passes direction.
2. **Roles.** Target shares come from the current personnel only:
   - PIT: Weeks 5-8, after Pittman went on IR and Roman Wilson got hurt.
   - CIN: the last 4 games plus the season, excluding Chase's 20%-snap Week 4.
   - The carry split comes from the games since Dowdle returned.
   - Shares vary from game to game (Dirichlet-multinomial).
3. **Efficiency.** I bootstrapped yards per catch and per carry from each player's own 2025-26 plays. For CIN receivers I used 2024 plus 2026, the healthy-Burrow seasons, so the Flacco/Browning 2025 sample does not drag them down. I also added about 10% random uncertainty to each player's efficiency.
4. **Matchups.** I took each defense's 2026 numbers allowed by position (8 games each) and shrunk them about halfway to the league average:
   - CIN allows 1.46x the league average in TE receiving yards and 1.20x in yards per carry.
   - PIT allows 0.87x in yards per carry and 1.48x in RB receiving yards.
5. **Simulation and windows.** I ran a 40,000-game Monte Carlo, including a 3% chance that each player leaves the game early. Windows come from the simulated quantiles, adjusted by judgment for the QBs.
6. **Calibration check.** I checked the widths against an empirical table built from 2023-2025 player-games: actual outcome quantiles grouped by each player's trailing average. That table gives a 50% window of roughly 0.55x to 1.35x the mean for a 60-yard receiver, and the windows here are about that wide on purpose.
7. **Consistency check.** Projected team receiving yards add up to each QB's projection: about 225 for PIT and about 255 for CIN.

**Three biggest uncertainties:**
1. **Game script and CIN pass volume.** Burrow has thrown anywhere from 29 to 54 times. An early PIT lead, or a shootout, moves every CIN receiving number and Perine's carries together. The same goes for PIT's run/pass mix: they ran 28-33 times when leading and passed 40-49 times when chasing.
2. **PIT tight ends and the Freiermuth illness.** If he is inactive or limited, Washington gains about 1-2 targets and 10-15 yards, and Freiermuth's numbers fall below these windows. If Freiermuth is inactive, his rows should be void, not graded.
3. **Efficiency outliers that should cool, plus the PIT backfield split:**
   - Metcalf's 52% catch rate and 5.9 yards per target.
   - Higgins' 15.8 yards per catch.
   - Warren's 5.9 yards per carry.
   - Warren and Dowdle split about 58/47% of the snaps last game, so a hot hand could move 30 or more rushing yards between them.
